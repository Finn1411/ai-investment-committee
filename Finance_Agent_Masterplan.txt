FINANCE AGENT – PROFESSIONAL MASTERPLAN

Your original approach already has a good basic architecture: Data → specialized analysts → Committee → Automation. However, for a truly professionally usable private Finance Agent, the system should not primarily attempt to predict exact stock prices. The goal should instead be a reproducible research and decision system that generates probabilistic scenarios, tests them against historical data, and continuously measures its own quality.

Guiding Principle:
* Not: "LLMs predict which stock will rise."
* But rather: "A data-driven investment research system evaluates probabilities, risks, scenarios, and the quality of its own forecasts."

======================================================================
PHASE 0 – GOAL DEFINITION & MEASUREMENT SYSTEM
======================================================================

Before the first agent is built, the decision rules must be defined. 

For each analysis:
* Investment horizon: 3 months / 12 months / 3–5 years
* Benchmark: e.g., MSCI World, S&P 500, or a sector-specific index
* Target variable:
    * Expected return
    * Probability of an outperformance
    * Downside risk
* Maximum position size
* Maximum portfolio and sector exposure
* Clear definition of what "Buy", "Hold", and "Avoid" means

The system should not only store ratings but also automatically review each forecast later.

Example:
Prediction:
Ticker: AAPL
Date: 20.08.2026
Expected 12M Return: +14%
Probability Outperform Benchmark: 68%
Bear Case: -22%
Base Case: +12%
Bull Case: +35%
Confidence: 0.72

After 3 / 6 / 12 months, the forecast is automatically evaluated.

======================================================================
PHASE 1 – PROFESSIONAL DATA LAYER
======================================================================

The data architecture should not merely retrieve individual metrics, but provide a central, structured database.

Project Structure:
finance_agent/
├── data/
│   ├── market_data/
│   ├── fundamentals/
│   ├── earnings/
│   ├── macro/
│   ├── news/
│   └── documents/
├── agents/
├── models/
├── backtesting/
├── portfolio/
├── evaluation/
└── database/

1. Market Data (Per Stock):
* Open / High / Low / Close / Volume
* Returns over various timeframes
* Volatility
* Beta
* Drawdowns
* Relative strength
* Liquidity
* Valuation in historical comparison

2. Fundamentals:
* Profitability: Gross Margin, Operating Margin, Net Margin, ROIC, ROE
* Cash Flow: Free Cash Flow, FCF Margin, FCF Yield, Cash Conversion
* Balance Sheet Quality: Net Debt / EBITDA, Interest Coverage, Current Ratio, Debt Maturity
* Growth: Revenue CAGR, EPS CAGR, FCF CAGR, Margin development
* Valuation: P/E, Forward P/E, EV/EBIT, EV/EBITDA, Price/Sales, FCF Yield

3. Data Quality Agent:
Before any analyst receives data, a Data Quality Agent checks:
* Are data missing?
* Are units consistent?
* Are there obvious outliers?
* Was a split taken into account?
* Is the metric up-to-date?
* Do quarterly and annual data match?
* Which data are estimated and which are reported?

Basic Rule: LLMs should never directly interpret unvalidated raw data.

======================================================================
PHASE 2 – THE RESEARCH TEAM
======================================================================

Instead of just three agents, a professional research team should consist of specialized roles.

AGENT 1: FUNDAMENTAL ANALYST
* Task: "How healthy and economically strong is the company?"
* Evaluates: Business Quality, Financial Strength, Profitability Trends, core strengths, core weaknesses.

AGENT 2: VALUE ANALYST
* Analyzes: Historical valuation ranges, Peer Comparison, Reverse DCF, FCF Yield, Margin of Safety.
* Central Question: "What expectations are already priced into the current stock price?"

AGENT 3: GROWTH & BUSINESS ANALYST
* Analyzes: Revenue growth, TAM, Scalability, Pricing Power, Competitive advantages, Unit Economics, Management Execution.
* Important Distinction: Narrative Growth vs. Fundamental Growth.

AGENT 4: EARNINGS & CATALYST ANALYST
* Searches for possible drivers: Earnings Dates, new products, regulatory decisions, margin changes, Guidance Changes, stock buybacks, M&A, insider activity.

AGENT 5: RISK MANAGER
* Creates a structured Risk Register (e.g., Balance Sheet, Competition, Valuation Compression risks).
* Additionally evaluates: Bear Case, Maximum Historical Drawdown, Scenario analysis, Concentration risk, Sector and macro risk.

AGENT 6: BEAR ANALYST / DEVIL'S ADVOCATE
* This agent receives the positive analyses and has only one task: "Why could this investment thesis be wrong?"
* It must not simply summarize the other agents; it must actively try to refute the investment thesis.
* Goal: Reduce groupthink.

AGENT 7: PORTFOLIO MANAGER
* The task is not only: "Is this stock good?"
* But rather: "Does this stock make sense in the context of my existing portfolio?"
* Considers: Existing positions, correlations, sector weighting, country risk, factor exposure, position size.
* Basic Principle: An excellent stock can still be a bad portfolio decision.

======================================================================
PHASE 3 – QUANT LAYER BEFORE THE LLM
======================================================================

Mathematical calculations must not be taken over by the LLM.

Architecture:
RAW DATA
↓
Python / Quant Engine
↓
Deterministic Calculations
↓
Validated Metrics
↓
Agents interpret results

The Quant Engine calculates, for example:
* CAGR, Sharpe Ratio, Sortino Ratio, Maximum Drawdown, Beta.
* Historical valuation percentiles, DCF, Monte Carlo simulations, scenario analyses, factor exposure.

======================================================================
PHASE 4 – COMMITTEE SYSTEM
======================================================================

The Head of Committee should not decide freely and subjectively on Buy/Hold/Sell.

Instead:
Data Validation
↓
Fundamental / Growth / Value Analyst
↓
Risk Manager
↓
Bear Analyst
↓
Committee Engine
↓
Portfolio Manager
↓
Final Recommendation

The Committee Engine uses a transparent scoring system.
Example Weights: 
* Business Quality: 20%
* Financial Strength: 15%
* Valuation: 20%
* Growth: 15%
* Catalysts: 10%
* Risk: 15%
* Portfolio Fit: 5%

Important: These weights should later be verified by backtesting and not assumed to be permanently correct.

======================================================================
PHASE 5 – PROBABILITY FORECASTING INSTEAD OF EXACT PRICE PREDICTION
======================================================================

No output like: "Apple will cost 245 € in 12 months."

Instead, use a 12-Month Scenario Model:

Bear Case:
Probability: 25%
Expected Return: -28%

Base Case:
Probability: 50%
Expected Return: +12%

Bull Case:
Probability: 25%
Expected Return: +42%

Expected Value: +10.5%
Probability of Outperforming Benchmark: 64%
Probability of >20% Drawdown: 31%

The goal is probabilistic decision support instead of an apparently precise point forecast.

======================================================================
PHASE 6 – BACKTESTING & ANTI-SELF-DECEPTION
======================================================================

For each historical analysis, the following must be saved: 
timestamp, ticker, data available at that time, agent outputs, scores, final recommendation, confidence, expected return, benchmark.

Then various quality metrics are calculated:
1. Hit Rate: How often was the direction correct?
2. Alpha: Did the system beat the benchmark?
3. Calibration: If the agent indicates a probability of 70%, does the event historically actually occur in approximately 70% of cases?
4. Confidence Accuracy: Are high-confidence predictions actually better than low-confidence predictions?
   * Result: If an agent is overconfident (e.g., says 90% confidence, but is historically only correct 55% of the time), the confidence must be calibrated.

======================================================================
PHASE 7 – PREDICTION JOURNAL
======================================================================

Every investment decision receives an immutable ID. 
(e.g., PREDICTION #00421 | Ticker: MSFT | Date: 2026-08-20)

It logs the Thesis, Expected Return, Confidence, Key Risks, and Invalidation Criteria. After the Review Date, it records the ACTUAL RESULT (Return, Benchmark, Result, and Reason).

Goal: The system should recognize which types of errors occur repeatedly.

======================================================================
PHASE 8 – RAG WITH CLEAR SOURCE HIERARCHY
======================================================================

The RAG system should prioritize sources according to quality.

* TIER 1 – PRIMARY SOURCES: Annual reports, quarterly reports, earnings calls, investor presentations, regulatory filings.
* TIER 2 – HIGH-QUALITY SECONDARY SOURCES: Reputable financial news, industry reports.
* TIER 3 – MARKET OPINIONS: Analyst opinions, blogs, social media.

Every important claim should be stored structurally (claim, source, source date, source type, confidence) to ensure traceability of why the agent makes a certain statement.

======================================================================
THE MOST IMPORTANT DESIGN PRINCIPLE
======================================================================

The Agent should not be understood as an "AI Stock Predictor."
Better: AI Investment Research & Forecasting System.

The end product should not claim: "Buy stock X, it will rise."
But rather: "Based on the data available at the time of analysis, the estimated probability of an outperformance against Benchmark X over 12 months is Y%. The investment thesis is based on A, B, and C. It becomes invalid if D, E, or F occur."

The system thereby becomes:
* Measurable
* Auditable
* Reproducible
* Backtestable
* Less prone to hallucinations
* Continuously improvable over the long term

ULTIMATE GOAL
Do not build an agent that sounds as convincing as possible. Build a system that:
1. Collects high-quality data.
2. Automatically validates it.
3. Performs calculations deterministically.
4. Analyzes specialized perspectives separately.
5. Actively seeks counterarguments.
6. Delivers probabilities instead of apparently precise forecasts.
7. Saves every prediction.
8. Can later be measured against real results.
9. Recognizes errors and overconfidence.
10. Can thus be systematically improved over time.

Note: The system is intended as a private research and decision support and does not replace your own verification of data or professional investment advice.
